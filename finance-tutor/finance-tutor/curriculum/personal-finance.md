# UK personal finance

Focus: decisions. Each topic should leave the learner able to make or weigh a real choice they face.
All figures must be checked against current-tax-year sources (`sources.md`) before teaching.

Last adjusted: 2026-10-13, after the first diagnostic (see `learner-profile.md`).

Status key: NEXT = teach next · TODO = not yet taught · BRIEF = mostly known, quick check only
Each topic should fit in 1–3 short sessions.

## Part 1: Tax foundations for every decision

### 1. Scottish income tax, National Insurance and your payslip — NEXT
Why first: knowing your marginal rate drives the value of pension relief, the ISA-vs-pension choice and salary decisions. Diagnostic: knew the personal allowance and that Scotland has extra lower bands; gave the rUK higher rate (40) instead of the Scottish one; did not know the S tax code prefix or own band; unsure whether NI differs.
Learning outcomes:
- Name every Scottish band and rate for the current tax year, and say which band your own top slice of pay falls into
- Explain why NI is UK-wide and how it sits alongside Scottish income tax
- Read your payslip: tax code (including the S prefix), tax, NI, pension deduction — and spot an obviously wrong code
- Work out the take-home effect of a rounded £1,000 pay rise at your band
Sessions: 1–2

### 2. Pension tax: relief on the way in, tax on the way out
Why: diagnostic showed a key misconception — believed pension withdrawals can be "all tax free over time". Must be fixed before any pension decision.
Prerequisite: topic 1 (marginal rates).
Learning outcomes:
- Explain how tax relief works on contributions (net pay / salary deduction vs relief at source) and what it's worth at your Scottish band
- Explain how pension income is taxed in retirement and how much can normally be taken tax-free (current rules and allowances)
- Explain how the tax-free lump sum works differently in a DB scheme (commutation) vs a DC pot
Sessions: 1–2

### 3. Where should the next £1 go? ISA vs pension vs mortgage vs cash
Why: the learner's core decision. Diagnostic: sensible ordering (emergency fund → ISA → pension → mortgage) justified only by access; did not weigh tax relief, guaranteed vs uncertain returns, or what would flip the order.
Prerequisite: topics 1–2.
Learning outcomes:
- Compare ISA, pension and general account on tax in, growth, tax out and access age
- Treat a mortgage overpayment as a guaranteed, risk-free return and compare it with expected investment returns
- State the conditions that would flip your own order (marginal rate, retirement-age plans, mortgage rate, need for access)
- Write down your own rule for where surplus money goes
Sessions: 2

### 4. ISA rules in detail
Diagnostic: knew the £20,000 overall allowance and that it can be split; aware of the planned Cash ISA limit change (exact figure and start date to be verified); listed SIPP as an ISA (it is a pension); missed Cash ISA and Innovative Finance ISA.
Learning outcomes:
- List the ISA types and the current allowance rules, including the LISA limit and how it counts towards the total
- Explain the planned Cash ISA changes and what (if anything) they change for you — VERIFY against gov.uk first
- Explain the difference between an ISA and a SIPP
Sessions: 1

## Part 2: NHS pension and retirement decisions

### 5. NHS 2015 scheme — consolidation — BRIEF
Diagnostic: strong. Correctly described 1/54th accrual of pensionable earnings, CPI + 1.5% revaluation while active, unfunded DB, and contrast with DC. Confirm in a second session before rating SECURE.
Learning outcomes:
- Estimate a year's accrual from a rounded salary and project it forward
- Explain what happens to revaluation after you leave the scheme
- Find and read your annual benefit statement (Total Reward Statement)
Sessions: 1 (short)

### 6. Retiring early from the NHS scheme
Why: likely the biggest single decision for the learner, and a complete gap in the diagnostic.
Prerequisite: topics 2 and 5.
Learning outcomes:
- Explain Normal Pension Age in the 2015 scheme and the early retirement reduction if you take benefits before it
- Explain the options for reducing that cut (for example buying out the reduction) and what they cost
- Sketch a bridging plan from an early retirement age to State Pension Age using ISA, DC pots and the NHS pension
Sessions: 2–3

### 7. State Pension
Diagnostic: knew it depends on NI contributions and that the forecast is on gov.uk; has not checked own forecast.
Learning outcomes:
- Explain qualifying years and what counts towards the full new State Pension
- Check your forecast and NI record, and judge whether filling any gap with voluntary contributions is worth it
- Explain the State Pension Age that applies to you
Sessions: 1
Action before this topic: check your forecast on gov.uk.

### 8. Extra pension saving as an NHS member
Learning outcomes:
- Compare NHS Additional Pension, Early Retirement Reduction Buy-Out, AVCs and a SIPP for extra retirement saving
- Pick the one that best fits an early-retirement goal and explain why
Sessions: 1–2

### 9. Pension allowances
Learning outcomes:
- Explain the annual allowance and how DB growth is measured against it, and when it could affect you
- Explain the lump sum allowances that replaced the lifetime allowance
Sessions: 1

### 11. Asset allocation and diversification
Learning outcomes:

- Set a target allocation that fits your timeline to early retirement

### 12. Investing outside a wrapper: CGT, dividend tax and bed-and-ISA
Diagnostic: knew CGT applies to gains; missed dividend tax; did not know ways to reduce the tax.
Learning outcomes:
- Explain CGT and dividend tax on a general account, with current allowances
- Use the annual exempt amount, bed-and-ISA and spouse transfers to reduce tax over time
Sessions: 1–2

### 13. Platforms and practicalities
Learning outcomes:
- Compare platform charging models (percentage vs flat fee) for your pot size
- Explain FSCS protection and what it does and doesn't cover

### 14. Behavioural rules and a written investment policy
Diagnostic: good instincts in a crash (keep contributing, keep buying, look for opportunities) but no pre-set rule.
Learning outcomes:
- Write a one-page investment policy: target allocation, rebalancing rule, what you do in a 30% fall
- Name the main biases that hit DIY investors and a rule against each

## Part 4: Big life decisions

### 15. Mortgages: overpay vs invest, remortgaging
Prerequisite: topic 3.
Diagnostic: SHAKY — compared rates in the right direction but did not frame overpaying as a risk-free return or weigh other factors.
Learning outcomes:
- Decide between overpaying and investing for a given mortgage rate, using risk, access and tax
- Explain overpayment limits, early repayment charges and how a lower loan-to-value can cut the rate at remortgage
Sessions: 1–2

### 16. Protection: income protection, life and critical illness
Diagnostic: knew NHS sick pay (6 months full + 6 months half, which applies with about 5 years' service) and SSP; knew such insurance exists but didn't name it.
Learning outcomes:
- Work out your own income gap if you couldn't work for 1 or 2+ years
- Choose an income protection deferred period that matches your sick pay
- Decide whether life or critical illness cover is needed, including NHS death-in-service benefits
Sessions: 1–2

### 17. Wills, inheritance tax and estate basics (Scots law)
Learning outcomes:
- Explain why a will matters under Scots law (including legal rights) and how pension nominations work
- Explain the IHT basics and how pensions are treated
Sessions: 1–2

### 18. Cash flow, budgeting and emergency funds — BRIEF
Diagnostic: emergency fund already built. Quick check on size and where it's held only.
Sessions: 1 (short), or fold into topic 19
